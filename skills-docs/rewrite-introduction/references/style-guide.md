# Style Guide for Rewriting Academic Introductions

Editorial reference: polished long-form magazine prose, adapted for academic writing.

## Core objective

Rewrite for clarity, flow, precision, and force.
The prose should feel written by an excellent editor: controlled, readable, intelligent, and clean.
Do not make it journalistic, breezy, or informal. It must remain recognizably academic.

---

## What to optimize

### 1. Lead with a real intellectual problem

Open with a concrete puzzle, contrast, or tension.
Avoid generic throat-clearing such as:

- "This paper studies..."
- "There is a large literature on..."
- "Scholars have long been interested in..."

The first paragraph should make the reader feel that something specific is at stake.

### 2. Prefer sharp, declarative sentences

Use direct syntax.
Prefer:

- "The puzzle is..."
- "Existing accounts miss..."
- "This argument implies..."

Avoid inflated constructions:

- "It is important to note that..."
- "It should be emphasized that..."
- "In order to understand..."

### 3. Cut abstraction when possible

Replace vague conceptual language with concrete formulation.
Prefer:

- "States join when membership expands market access"

over:

- "Ratification incentives are shaped by positive externalities embedded in institutional participation"

Abstraction is acceptable only when analytically necessary.

### 4. Preserve the paper's real contribution

Do not oversell.
Do not claim novelty beyond what the text can support.
If prior literature already addresses part of the issue, state the narrower contribution precisely.

### 5. Keep the causal and theoretical logic explicit

The reader should never have to infer the mechanism.
State clearly:

- what varies
- what causes what
- why
- under what conditions

### 6. Improve rhythm and pacing

Vary sentence length.
Do not stack several long, heavily subordinated sentences in sequence.
Break dense argument into readable units.
A paragraph should usually do one main job.

### 7. Eliminate filler and bureaucratic prose

Delete phrases that add no content, such as:

- "in the context of"
- "it is worth noting"
- "it is important to highlight"
- "plays an important role"
- "has implications for our understanding of"

Replace with precise claims.

### 8. Avoid unnecessary metadiscourse

Do not overuse:

- "The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows"
- "This paper is organized in the following way"
- "In this section, I will show"

Use only if needed and keep brief.

### 9. Retain disciplinary seriousness

The tone should be elegant, not chatty.
No rhetorical questions unless they genuinely sharpen the puzzle.
No slang.
No magazine-style flourish that weakens precision.

### 10. Favor nouns and verbs over adjective-heavy prose

Keep modifiers disciplined.
Prefer strong verbs to decorative adjectives.

---

## Paragraph-level structure for an introduction

A strong introduction will usually do most of the following in sequence:

1. Present a concrete puzzle or motivating contrast.
2. State the argument in plain but precise language.
3. Clarify the mechanism or logic.
4. Explain what existing literature misses or conflates.
5. State the contribution narrowly and credibly.
6. Preview the empirical or formal payoff.

---

## Sentence-level preferences

### Prefer

- shorter openings
- active voice when it improves clarity
- concrete nouns
- verbs that carry analytical meaning
- explicit contrasts where analytically needed

### Avoid

- excessive passive voice
- stacked nominalizations
- long parenthetical interruptions
- repeated use of "indeed", "moreover", "furthermore"
- empty intensifiers such as "very", "quite", "highly" unless substantively meaningful

---

## Treatment of literature

Engage literature selectively and strategically.
Do not front-load the introduction with a long literature review.
Mention prior work only where it helps define:

- the puzzle
- the gap
- the mistaken assumption
- the contribution

Literature discussion should sharpen the argument, not perform coverage.

---

## Treatment of claims

When revising, distinguish clearly among:

- descriptive claim
- causal claim
- theoretical claim
- methodological claim

Do not blur them together in a single overloaded sentence.

---

## Treatment of novelty

Never say:

- "This is the first paper to..."

unless the draft provides strong support.

Prefer:

- "The paper shows..."
- "The paper clarifies..."
- "The paper reframes..."
- "The paper identifies a condition under which..."

---

## House style

- Clear, compressed, serious prose
- Elegant but unsentimental
- No hype
- No clichés
- No generic signposting
- No inflated claims
- No unnecessary jargon
- Keep technical terms when they are doing real work
- If a simpler word preserves meaning, use it

---

## Addendum for Political Science / IR introductions

- The opening should identify a substantive political or international puzzle, not merely a literature gap.
- The central claim should be stated early and in plain language.
- Distinguish clearly between the empirical pattern to be explained and the mechanism that explains it.
- Do not present "the literature" as monolithic if the field is actually divided.
- Avoid saying the paper "contributes to several literatures" unless each contribution is real and specific.
- If the paper contains a formal model, explain its intuition in readable prose before emphasizing derivations.
- If the paper contains empirical evidence, explain what the evidence is evidence of.
- If identification is important, state why the design supports the inference without lapsing into methods-jargon overload.
- The introduction should make clear why the result matters for how scholars interpret a broader class of outcomes, not just the specific case.

---

## Avoid AI/LLM writing tics

The revised prose should not sound like generic AI-assisted writing.
It should read like a sharp human editor worked carefully on the text.

### Do not use these contrastive crutches

Avoid formulae such as:

- "It is not X, but Y"
- "The issue is not whether..., but how..."
- "This is not simply..., it is..."
- "Not only..., but also..." when used for rhetorical lift rather than analytical necessity
- "Rather than..., the paper shows..."
- "The point is not that..., but that..."

Use these only when the logic genuinely requires a contrast.
Do not use them as a default sentence template.

### Do not overuse emphatic pivots

Use sparingly:

- however
- indeed
- in fact
- rather
- instead
- crucially
- importantly
- notably

These words often accumulate in AI-generated prose and make the rhythm feel programmatic.

### Avoid excessive typographic signaling

- Do not use bold for emphasis in the prose.
- Do not use italics for rhetorical stress unless technically required.
- Do not use em dashes repeatedly to create drama or pseudo-elegance.
- Do not use colon-heavy sentences as a substitute for paragraph structure.
- Do not use semicolons excessively to simulate sophistication.

Typography should not do the work of argument.

### Avoid over-patterned sentence construction

Do not produce strings of sentences with the same cadence, such as:

- short setup sentence
- abstract contrast sentence
- punchy conclusion sentence

Vary rhythm naturally.
The prose should not sound templated.

### Avoid generic "smart-sounding" phrasing

Do not rely on phrases like:

- "at the heart of"
- "speaks to"
- "sheds light on"
- "helps us understand"
- "complicates the view that"
- "reveals an important insight"
- "underscores the importance of"
- "raises broader questions about"

Prefer direct statement of the actual claim.

### Avoid synthetic paragraph endings

Do not end paragraphs with sweeping summary lines that merely sound polished, such as:

- "This matters far beyond the case at hand."
- "The implication is broader than it first appears."
- "The stakes are therefore both empirical and theoretical."

Paragraph endings should advance substance, not perform significance.

### Avoid inflated coherence

Do not make every transition overly smooth.
A real scholarly introduction can move briskly and directly.
Do not insert transition phrases merely to make the text sound polished.

### Avoid unnecessary symmetry

Do not default to neat triads and balanced formulations, such as:

- "conceptually, empirically, and normatively"
- "theoretically rich, empirically grounded, and methodologically rigorous"

Use symmetry only when it reflects real structure.

### Avoid empty compression

Do not replace a precise sentence with a shorter but vaguer one.
Concision is good only if analytical content is preserved.

### Avoid vague upgrade words

Use sparingly or not at all:

- robust
- nuanced
- compelling
- powerful
- striking
- important
- key
- central
- critical

These words often substitute for explanation.

### Avoid performative elegance

Do not make the prose sound literary for its own sake.
The goal is controlled, intelligent academic writing, not stylized polish.

---

## Punctuation discipline

- Use em dashes rarely.
- Prefer commas or full stops unless the dash is clearly the best syntactic choice.
- Do not stack parenthetical interruptions.
- Do not use ellipses.
- Do not use bullet-point logic inside running prose.
- Avoid repeated sentence openings with the same connective structure.
- Prefer clean declarative sentences over punctuation-driven emphasis.

---

## Formatting discipline

- No boldface for argumentative emphasis.
- No italicized stress words.
- No ALL CAPS.
- No scare quotes unless analytically necessary.
- No numbered contribution list unless the journal style or the author explicitly wants it.

---

## Do not do the following

- Do not turn the prose into magazine journalism.
- Do not add rhetorical flourish that is not in service of argument.
- Do not simplify technical claims to the point of imprecision.
- Do not exaggerate novelty.
- Do not add examples not present in the original unless explicitly asked.
- Do not replace precise disciplinary terms with generic language.
- Do not make the author sound like a public-facing op-ed writer.
- Do not use generic AI-style contrast formulas repeatedly.
- Do not use typography or punctuation-based emphasis to simulate force.
- Do not produce overly symmetrical, overly polished, or overly packaged prose.
- Do not use stock phrases that sound intelligent but add little content.

---

## Non-negotiables

Do not alter:

- substantive meaning
- citations
- formal claims
- scope conditions
- empirical findings
- terminology that has a precise technical role

Do not invent literature, evidence, or implications.

---

## Output requirements

When rewriting:

1. Preserve the author's argument.
2. Improve clarity, flow, and force.
3. Make the introduction more readable and more memorable.
4. Keep it suitable for a top academic journal.
5. Return prose that sounds like a first-rate human editor revised it.

---

## Final test

The rewritten introduction should satisfy all of the following:

- A smart reader can understand the puzzle quickly.
- The contribution is clear by the end of page 1.
- The prose sounds controlled, not mechanical.
- The writing is more vivid, but no less rigorous.
- Every paragraph has a job.

---

## Final anti-LLM check

Before finalizing, check:

- Does any sentence sound like a reusable AI template?
- Are there repeated contrast formulas?
- Are there too many pivot words like "however" or "indeed"?
- Is emphasis being created by typography rather than argument?
- Does the prose sound overly balanced, symmetrical, or pre-packaged?

If yes, rewrite for greater naturalness and specificity.
